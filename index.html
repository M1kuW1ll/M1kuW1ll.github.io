About me

I am a fourth-year (final-year) PhD student in Computer Science at the Department of Informatics, King's College London, supervised by Dr. Stefanos Leonardos and Prof. Carmine Ventre. Before my PhD, I obtained my MSc in Computer Science at the University of Southampton.

My research centers on Decentralized Finance (DeFi) and crypto-economics. I currently focus on the economics of Maximal Extractable Value (MEV) and Ethereum protocol design, combining game theory, agent-based modeling and simulation, empirical data analysis, and mechanism design.

I am fortunate to be advised by researchers from the (former) Robust Incentives Group of the Ethereum Foundation and Flashbots.

Research interests

Cryptoeconomics

Incentives and mechanism design in blockchain protocols: proposer-builder separation, block-building auctions and fee markets, studied with auction theory and game theory.

  • PBS & ePBS
  • Block building
  • Fee markets
  • Auction theory
  • Mechanism design

Decentralized finance

Maximal extractable value and arbitrage: how searchers compete for MEV, from CEX-DEX arbitrage on Ethereum to probing on high-throughput chains.

  • MEV
  • CEX-DEX arbitrage
  • Searcher competition
  • On-chain data
